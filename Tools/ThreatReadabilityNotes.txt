Behaviour and tuning
Grappler: 1.1-second preparation; aim locks for the final 0.35 seconds. A
committed ray can miss a sidestepping player or hit cover. Rewind lasts 1.2
seconds; the existing five-second attack cooldown also remains in effect.
Configure GrapplerThiefSettings.asset in Settings/Thieves.

Rocket thief: 1.5-second preparation; aim locks for the final 0.45 seconds.
The thief stands still to prepare, fires towards the committed world point,
then pauses for a 1.5-second recovery. Existing fire cooldowns still apply.
Configure the Rocket Telegraph fields under Combat in
Settings/Thieves/RocketLauncherThiefSettings.asset.

Rogue car: approaches at 40% speed, lines up within 22 degrees of its target,
then brakes for a 1.8-second warning. Its bearing is sampled when preparation
starts; the final 0.6 seconds explicitly enters the locked phase. The charge
lasts two seconds, cannot reverse or retarget, and caps steering at 12 degrees
per second. Braking recovery lasts 2.2 seconds. Scripted player impact damage
is enabled only during the committed charge. Physical chassis collisions
remain solid. Configure Charge Telegraph fields in the Medium/Hard delivery
RogueCarSettings assets. Getaway trucks keep their existing motor behaviour.

Presentation
Each enemy prefab has a ThreatTelegraph component with editable source,
clip, preparation signal, bearing arrow and recovery signal references.
Yellow shapes identify preparation; blue pause bars identify recovery.
Shape differences supplement colour: hook, ignition symbol, charge chevron.
The arrows show an attack bearing, not an explosion radius or guaranteed
trajectory. They are solid scene meshes and are occluded by scenery.
The grappler reel and rogue roof identifier are hidden during destruction
or ragdoll/launch states. The launcher retains its existing weapon silhouette.

Arrival sounds play once per local client when a threat first enters the
configured audible distance. Separate spatial voices carry preparation,
commitment and recovery sounds. Thirteen original synthesised cue clips are
saved in Assets/_Project/Audio/Threats; replace them through the Inspector.
Preparation audio follows the configured wind-up duration. Late clients
seek into the current cue instead of restarting its sound or deadline.
The shared rocket projectile now has a saved looping flight voice, so player
rockets also use that whistle. It starts/stops with the client spawn lifecycle.

Networking
The server owns attack decisions. A single synchronised snapshot carries
the phase, start/end ticks, target and locked point. Navigation pauses during
thief preparation/recovery. Death, knockback and departure cancel queued
attacks through the existing combat/health paths. No runtime warning object
construction or prefab rebuilding is used.

Validation
Unity menu: Couch Guys > Tests > Threat Readability > Validate Assets
Unity menu: Couch Guys > Tests > Threat Readability > Run Multiplayer Probe
The probe uses a local host plus an independent late client, checks shared
deadlines, locks, recovery/cancellation, grapple dodges and cover, rocket
commitment and flight audio, and rogue damage/charge commitment.
Results: Tools/ThreatReadabilityValidation.txt
Preview: Tools/ThreatReadabilityPreview.png
The probe restores the Editor scene by leaving its temporary Play session.
It does not simulate internet latency or replace multiplayer balance playtests.

Tools/author_threat_readability.py is an explicit one-time asset authoring
utility. It skips already-authored cues to preserve Inspector customisations.
Nothing invokes it on import, script reload or entering Play mode.
